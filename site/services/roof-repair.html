Roof repair

Targeted fixes for leaks, damaged shingles, flashing, and ventilation — without pushing a full replacement you don't need.

Common repair issues

  • Active leaks. Often traced to flashing around chimneys, skylights, or vent pipes rather than the shingle field itself.
  • Missing or damaged shingles. Wind can lift or tear individual shingles without affecting the rest of the roof.
  • Flashing failures. Rusted, loose, or improperly sealed flashing is one of the most common sources of leaks we find that isn't obvious from the ground.
  • Ventilation problems. Poor attic ventilation can cause premature shingle wear and ice damming in winter, which looks like a roofing problem but is really an airflow problem.

Repair or replacement?

As a rule of thumb: if the damage is isolated to one area and the rest of the roof is in reasonable shape for its age, repair is usually the right call. If we're finding the same type of damage in multiple areas, or the roof is already near the end of its expected lifespan, we'll say so plainly and point you to our replacement page instead of quoting a repair that won't hold up.

What a repair visit looks like

  1. Inspection. We identify the source of the issue — not just the spot where water is showing up inside, which is often some distance from the actual entry point.
  2. Written scope. A specific description of what will be repaired and the price, before any work starts.
  3. Repair. Most single-issue repairs are completed in a few hours, depending on access and the extent of the damage found.
  4. Follow-up. If the repair was related to an interior leak, we recommend checking the area after the next heavy rain and will come back out if it isn't fully resolved.

A note on patch jobs

If a roof has already been patched multiple times in the same spot, that's often a sign the underlying cause hasn't been addressed — we'll tell you if that's what we're seeing rather than applying another patch over it.
