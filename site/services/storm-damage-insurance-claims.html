Storm damage & insurance claim support

Hail and wind damage after a storm event — documented clearly, explained plainly, with no pressure tactics.

What we can do: inspect your roof, document damage with photos and notes, meet your insurance adjuster on-site to point out what we found, and provide a repair/replacement estimate your insurer can use. What we can't do: approve your claim, guarantee a payout, or speak for your insurance company. The insurer makes the coverage decision — we make sure the facts in front of them are accurate and complete.

The process, step by step

1. Free storm inspection

We check for the signs insurers look for: granule loss and shingle bruising, dented soft metals (vents, gutters, flashing — these dent before shingles usually show visible damage), and any interior leak evidence in the attic. You get a written summary either way, including if we find nothing worth claiming.

2. Decide whether to file

If we find damage consistent with a storm event, we'll tell you what we found and let you decide whether to call your insurer. We don't file the claim for you, and we won't tell you storm damage exists if it doesn't.

3. Adjuster meeting

Once you've opened a claim, your insurance company sends an adjuster to inspect the roof. We ask to be on-site for that inspection so we can point out anything that might otherwise be missed from the ground — we do not control what the adjuster writes up.

4. Scope & estimate review

The adjuster's report becomes the basis for your insurer's settlement offer. If our inspection found damage the adjuster's scope doesn't cover, we can prepare a supplement request with photo evidence for your insurer to review — this is a documentation step, not a guarantee the insurer will add it.

5. Repair or replacement

Once your claim is settled, we schedule the work using the agreed scope. If you're paying out of pocket for upgrades beyond what insurance covers (for example, upgrading shingle class), that's itemized separately and agreed to in writing before we start.

What your policy typically covers

Most homeowners' policies cover sudden storm damage (hail, wind) minus your deductible, which is the amount you owe regardless of the contractor you choose. Gradual wear, poor maintenance, and pre-existing damage are generally not covered — and a reputable contractor should tell you plainly if that's what they find, rather than suggesting it's stormrelated when it isn't.

Questions worth asking any roofer about your claim

  • Will you be on-site for my adjuster meeting, and is that included in your estimate?
  • Do you have a local address and a license number I can verify?
  • What happens if my insurer denies the claim — do I still owe you for the inspection?
  • Can I get the scope of work and price in writing before you start?

Answering "no" to the first two, or hesitating on the last two, is a reasonable reason to call a different contractor — including us, if you'd like a second opinion.
