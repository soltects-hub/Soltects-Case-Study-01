Request a Free Inspection

What we can do: inspect your roof, document damage with photos and notes, meet your insurance adjuster on-site to point out what we found, and provide a repair/replacement estimate your insurer can use. What we can't do: approve your claim, guarantee a payout, or speak for your insurance company. The insurer makes the coverage decision — we make sure the facts in front of them are accurate and complete.

Step by step

The claims process, honestly explained.

1. Free storm inspection

We check for the signs insurers look for: granule loss, shingle bruising, dented soft metals, and attic leak evidence. You get a written summary either way, including if we find nothing worth claiming.

2. Decide whether to file

If we find damage consistent with a storm event, we'll tell you what we found and let you decide whether to call your insurer. We won't tell you storm damage exists if it doesn't.

3. Adjuster meeting

Once you've opened a claim, we ask to be on-site for the adjuster's inspection to point out anything that might be missed from the ground. We do not control what the adjuster writes up.

4. Scope & estimate review

The adjuster's report becomes the basis for your insurer's settlement offer. If our inspection found damage the scope doesn't cover, we can prepare a supplement request with photo evidence — a documentation step, not a guarantee the insurer adds it.

5. Repair or replacement

Once your claim is settled, we schedule the work using the agreed scope. Upgrades beyond what insurance covers are itemized separately and agreed to in writing before we start.

Coverage

What your policy typically covers.

Most homeowners' policies cover sudden storm damage (hail, wind) minus your deductible, which is the amount you owe regardless of the contractor you choose. Gradual wear, poor maintenance, and pre-existing damage are generally not covered — and a reputable contractor should tell you plainly if that's what they find, rather than suggesting it's storm-related when it isn't.

Worth asking

Questions worth asking any roofer about your claim.

  • Will you be on-site for my adjuster meeting, and is that included in your estimate?
  • Do you have a local address and a license number I can verify?
  • What happens if my insurer denies the claim — do I still owe you for the inspection?
  • Can I get the scope of work and price in writing before you start?

Answering "no" to the first two, or hesitating on the last two, is a reasonable reason to call a different contractor — including us, if you'd like a second opinion.
